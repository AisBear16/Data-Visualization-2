Roll the Dice: The World of Board Games

From CATAN to Monopoly, board games bring friends and families around the same table. But who plays them around the world, which kinds are most popular, and are the hardest games really the best?

Part 1

Where the world's board gamers are

BoardGameGeek is the world's largest board game website, where players rate games and log their collections. More than 400,000 of its members say which country they live in, which gives us a map of the hobby.

The United States alone has about 155,000 members. Malaysia has 765, most of them in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur.

Big countries have more members simply because they have more people, so this map shows members per 100,000 people. Canada (77) and the Nordic countries lead. Malaysia has about 2.4 per 100,000, so the hobby is still small here, with plenty of room to grow.

89%

of members with a listed country live in North America or Europe. Asia, home to about 59% of the world's people, has just 4%.

Trade data tells a similar story. The United States imported over US$12 billion of board and table games in 2023, about four times more than Germany. Malaysia imported US$41 million. (Trade code HS 950490 also includes casino and arcade table games, not only board games.)

Part 2

What kinds of games are out there?

BoardGameGeek sorts games into eight broad categories, such as family games, party games and strategy games. "Wargames" recreate battles, and "thematic" games tell a story.

Releases of ranked games more than doubled from 2000 to 2010 and peaked at 529 in 2015. Data on categories stops at 2020.

Part 3

Is harder better?

Each game has a complexity score, also called its "weight". Players vote from 1 (simple rules) to 5 (many rules and long-term planning). The score is the average of those votes.

71% of games score under 2.5, so most games are light to medium.

Games with a complexity of 4 or more average 7.5 out of 10, while games under 2 average 6.0. Players who rate games on BoardGameGeek seem to reward depth.

Part 4

What makes a hit?

The best-rated games and the most-owned games are not the same. The top-ranked games are long and complex, while the games in the most homes are quick and easy to learn.

The typical top-25 game takes 2 hours, suits ages 13 and up and has a complexity of about 3.6.

Pandemic, CATAN and Carcassonne are each owned by about 150,000 members, and all three have a complexity of 2.4 or less, far lighter than the top-ranked games.
